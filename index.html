Yongwen Zhang (张永文) Research Group

Complex Systems Science

How does collective behavior emerge in the Earth system, and what can it teach us about complex systems more broadly? Statistical physics provides our main theoretical framework and methods for tracing the patterns we observe back to the interactions that produce them. In turn, we take questions raised by Earth observations as a starting point for developing new theories in complex systems science and statistical physics.

Explore our research ↘

Research

Statistical physics of the Earth system

How do interactions among the ocean, atmosphere and land give rise to patterns that span entire regions or persist across seasons? We combine Earth observations with network analysis and physical models to identify which interactions and feedbacks shape the behavior we see at larger scales.

Collective behavior and nonequilibrium dynamics

The Earth system poses fundamental questions about systems far from equilibrium. For instance, why can the same disturbance produce different responses depending on the state of the system? We use simplified models and numerical experiments to work out which mechanisms matter and whether the resulting principles hold in other complex systems.

People

Team Leader

Yongwen Zhang

Yongwen Zhang is an associate professor at Kunming University of Science and Technology. He studied theoretical physics at the Institute of Theoretical Physics, Chinese Academy of Sciences, and carried out postdoctoral research at Bar-Ilan University. He uses statistical physics to study emergence in the Earth system. He is particularly interested in what Earth observations can teach us about the general principles governing complex systems away from equilibrium.

1 September 2026

What can Earth system science and complex systems science learn from each other? ↗

In a perspective in Fundamental Research, Yongwen Zhang, Deliang Chen and Jingfang Fan trace a two-way exchange through the idea of emergence. Concepts such as phase transitions help explain how abrupt changes can arise on Earth. In turn, observations of how Earth responds to disturbances suggest new directions for the theory of systems far from equilibrium.
